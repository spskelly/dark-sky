    check before you go

    this list is one person's notes, not an official source, and it is not kept in sync with anybody's rulebook. coordinates are approximate parking or summit points, drive times are estimated from a straight line rather than routed and read low wherever the road has to go around a ridge, and the camping, fire and access notes were true when they were written and may not be true tonight. gates close for ice, forest orders change, permits get introduced, roads wash out, and campgrounds run seasonally.

    confirm anything you are relying on with whoever manages the land, and never plan a night around a single line of text here: parkway road closures, smokies road status, national forests in nc, nc state parks.

    these are remote places at four to six thousand feet, often on gravel, usually with no signal. weather turns faster up there than the forecast suggests and a closed gate at 9pm is a long detour. you are responsible for your own safety and for knowing the rules where you park and sleep.

    reading the mountains

    • parkway gates. the high sections between Mount Pisgah and Waterrock Knob close for ice and snow, often for weeks at a time from November into April. check the NPS road status page before driving up; a closed gate at 9pm is a long detour.
    • milky way core. at this latitude the galactic center sits in the southern sky. it clears the ridge before dawn from late February, is up most of the night by June, and is an evening object August through October. each window in the list above says which.
    • south-facing matters. the core rises in the southeast and sets in the southwest, low. overlooks that face south or west across a big valley (Cowee, Devil's Courthouse, Cherohala) beat 360-degree summits with a nearby ridge to the south.
    • fog and dew. valleys fog in after midnight most of the year; ridgetops above 5,500 feet usually stay clear of it but everything gets wet. bring a lens warmer and expect the tent fly to be soaked.
    • temperature. subtract about 15 degrees from whatever your valley forecast says for the 6,000-foot balds, and add wind. July nights on Black Balsam run in the 50s.
    • leaf season. October new moons are the best of the year for sky, and the worst for finding a parking spot at Graveyard Fields. arrive before sunset or go somewhere less famous.
    • forecasting. every spot links to its clear outside forecast: cloud cover by hour, plus transparency and seeing, which matter more than the daytime forecast suggests.

    phase times come from the meeus true-phase algorithm and are converted to your local time zone, so dates should match published almanacs to the day. the window is a planning heuristic: the moon also rises and sets, so a waxing crescent a few days past new is gone by late evening, and a waning crescent doesn't rise until the small hours. those edge days are often darker than they look. cloud cover comes from open-meteo and is a model, not a promise; the spot notes are hand-written and are not a substitute for checking with the land manager.
