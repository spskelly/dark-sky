    reading the mountains

    • parkway gates. the high sections between Mount Pisgah and Waterrock Knob close for ice and snow, often for weeks at a time from November into April. check the NPS road status page before driving up; a closed gate at 9pm is a long detour.
    • milky way core. at this latitude the galactic center sits in the southern sky. it clears the ridge before dawn from late February, is up most of the night by June, and is an evening object August through October. each window in the list above says which.
    • south-facing matters. the core rises in the southeast and sets in the southwest, low. overlooks that face south or west across a big valley (Cowee, Devil's Courthouse, Cherohala) beat 360-degree summits with a nearby ridge to the south.
    • fog and dew. valleys fog in after midnight most of the year; ridgetops above 5,500 feet usually stay clear of it but everything gets wet. bring a lens warmer and expect the tent fly to be soaked.
    • temperature. subtract about 15 degrees from the Waynesville forecast for the 6,000-foot balds, and add wind. July nights on Black Balsam run in the 50s.
    • leaf season. October new moons are the best of the year for sky, and the worst for finding a parking spot at Graveyard Fields. arrive before sunset or go somewhere less famous.
    • forecasting. every spot links to its clear outside forecast: cloud cover by hour, plus transparency and seeing, which matter more than the daytime forecast suggests.

    phase times come from the meeus true-phase algorithm and are converted to your local time zone, so dates should match published almanacs to the day. the window is a planning heuristic: the moon also rises and sets, so a waxing crescent a few days past new is gone by late evening, and a waning crescent doesn't rise until the small hours. those edge days are often darker than they look.
